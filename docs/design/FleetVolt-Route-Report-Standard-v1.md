# FleetVolt Route Report Standard v1.0.0

Status: **LOCKED**

Protocol: **Command Brief narrative / Corridor White Paper design**

Approved reference: POLB ↔ Shafter Command Brief Final

This is the protocol of record for every FleetVolt corridor or route report generated by the application, an agent, or a human collaborator.

## Audience and purpose

Write for executive, fleet, policy, planning, utility, infrastructure, and research stakeholders. Give the board answer first, then build a traceable evidence chain that distinguishes provider results, modeled outputs, assumptions, open gates, and recommendations.

## Locked report sequence

1. Cover and board answer
2. Executive finding
3. Corridor evidence
4. Vehicle and energy model
5. Charging landscape
6. Operational emissions
7. Origin-side carrier prospects
8. Destination-side carrier prospects
9. Commercial interpretation
10. Method, limitations, and authorization

Sections may expand when the evidence requires it, but may not be reordered or silently omitted. Missing evidence must be disclosed in its expected section.

## Narrative and evidence contract

- Use a decisive, research-analyst voice that is candid about uncertainty.
- Lead each section with the decision implication, then explain its evidence and boundary.
- Every material number and claim must be traceable to the evidence manifest.
- Label evidence `VERIFIED`, `MODELED`, `ASSUMPTION`, `SOURCE`, `GATE`, or `OPEN`.
- Never fabricate route data, charging capability, field observations, carrier details, contacts, or economics.
- Render missing facts as `Not verified` or `Research required`.
- Proximity creates a prospect list. It does not prove route use.
- Separate operational findings, planning scenarios, and deployment recommendations.

## Required analysis

Every full route report addresses freight demand; truck traffic; route geometry; modeled vehicle configuration, efficiency, battery and arrival state of charge; current charging; anticipated charging events; infrastructure gaps; carrier proximity; commercial significance; assumptions; exclusions; next actions; and authorization gates.

### Mandatory diesel-versus-BEV comparison

Compare a standard diesel Class-8 tractor and the modeled battery-electric Class-8 tractor over equal work and equal distance. State the trip/cycle, diesel fuel economy and factor, BEV energy intensity, battery and reserve, charging efficiency, grid energy, grid factor/date, tailpipe and grid-operational results, avoided emissions, boundary, and exclusions. Tailpipe-only, grid-operational, well-to-wheel, and lifecycle values are not interchangeable.

## Carrier-table contract

Use separate, fully ruled origin-side and destination-side tables. Columns: Carrier; City/proximity; Tractors; Website; Contact; Phone; Email; Verification/route-use gate. Populate only verified attributes; otherwise write `Not verified`. Do not expose internal carrier keys.

## Visual contract

- Warm paper and restrained editorial composition.
- Editorial analysis in Georgia/Times; technical labels in Arcline/Arial.
- Paper logo: black `FLEET`, blue `VOLT`, blue bolt.
- Road-geometry route maps with fully visible endpoint labels.
- Restrained blue, copper, and green accents that encode meaning.
- Fully ruled tables, never semicolon-delimited prospect prose.
- Intentional whitespace; incomplete empty regions are defects.

## Release gates

1. Validate every material number against the evidence manifest.
2. Verify the equal-work/equal-distance emissions math and boundary.
3. Explicitly label every unverified carrier field.
4. Generate complete HTML and PDF artifacts.
5. Render every PDF page and inspect full-page and readable zoom.
6. Reject clipping, overflow, footer collisions, broken borders, orphan headings/captions, obscured labels, or unfinished whitespace.
7. Record the report-standard version in artifact metadata.

## Version control

Version `1.0.0` is locked. Changes to narrative, sequence, evidence rules, emissions requirements, typography, palette, logo, or layout gates require a reviewed new version. Route facts are inputs—not changes to the standard.
