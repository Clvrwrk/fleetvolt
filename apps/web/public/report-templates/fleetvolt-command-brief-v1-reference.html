Executive finding

The decision is not whether the truck can reach Shafter. It can. The decision is where it recharges before returning.

PTV confirms a 305-mile round trip. FleetVolt's modeled intensity implies 656 kWh at the battery and 729 kWh from the grid, making a Shafter-side charge event the operating hinge of the corridor.

What the evidence supports

PTV returned Class-8 road geometry and directional drive time in both directions. Under the approved payload and operating assumptions, the modeled tractor completes either one-way leg with approximately 16.5% to 17.0% state of charge remaining.

The corridor is therefore feasible as a scheduled electric operation. It is not a one-charge turn, and it is not yet dispatch-ready because the energy model has not consumed the PTV grade trace and no Shafter-area destination site has passed access, power and detour acceptance.

Analyst interpretation

The operating path links the Port of Long Beach to a Central Valley freight market through I-710, I-5 and CA-99. FleetVolt's sampled Caltrans layer reports a mean of 6,098 five-axle trucks per day. That establishes heavy-duty relevance; it does not prove that every observed truck completes the POLB-Shafter movement.

FAF5 provides the regional demand frame: 393.4 million truck-mode tons valued at approximately $905.9 billion originated in the Los Angeles-Long Beach zone in 2023. Those figures describe the market surrounding the corridor, not a route-specific allocation. FleetVolt should therefore present freight intensity as context until telematics, gate or bill-of-lading evidence qualifies lane use.

How management should read the result

The modeled tractor is a Freightliner eCascadia configured with a 34,000-pound payload, ideal weather and port-drayage loads. FleetVolt currently simulates a 100-mile synthetic-flat route and returns 2.151 kWh per mile. This report applies that intensity to the 153-mile outbound and 152-mile return PTV road legs.

The result is deliberately more operational than quoting nominal vehicle range. Approximately 65 kWh remains at Shafter, leaving insufficient energy for the return. Grade, wind, temperature, congestion and auxiliary loads may materially change the requirement; those sensitivities belong in the next model run.

Operational emissions

Electric service cuts operational CO2 by 71.8% on the stated boundary.

Equal work, equal distance

A standard diesel Class-8 tractor at 6.5 mpg consumes 46.9 gallons over the 305-mile round trip. Using the EPA combustion factor of 10.18 kg CO2 per gallon, the trip produces 477.7 kg of tailpipe CO2.

The modeled BEV requires 656.2 kWh at the battery and approximately 729.1 kWh from the grid after charging losses. At EIA's 2024 California in-state generation rate of 407 lb CO2/MWh, that electricity represents 134.6 kg CO2.

Revenue logic

Every modeled truck performing one round trip on 250 operating days creates approximately 10.94 GWh of annual grid demand. This is a demand scenario, not a contracted-load forecast. Its monetizable event is the Shafter-side replenishment where a natural freight dwell can be converted into a controlled charging session.

The business case is strongest when FleetVolt controls the route model, energy forecast, site recommendation and customer qualification as one operating workflow. Selling a charger alone would leave the route risk with the customer.

Method, limitations and authorization

What is verified, what is modeled, and what must happen next.

PTV VERIFIED

153.0 miles outbound, 152.0 miles inbound, directional drive time, 800-point road geometry and a Class-8 truck profile.

CORRIDOR CONTEXT

Caltrans sampled five-axle AADT, FAF5 zone 061 freight totals and FleetVolt carrier proximity.

FLEETVOLT MODELED

2.151 kWh/mile at 34,000-pound payload, ideal weather, port drayage and conservative usable energy.

PLANNING ASSUMPTIONS

6.5 diesel mpg, 90% charging efficiency, 250 annual cycles and one round trip per truck-day.

Material limitations

  1. The PTV geometry and times are live provider results; the physics model does not yet consume the PTV polyline or grade profile.
  2. Energy is a transparent normalization of FleetVolt's modeled kWh/mile to PTV route miles under ideal weather and synthetic-flat elevation.
  3. Truck AADT is a sampled corridor mean; FAF freight totals are regional context, not route tonnage.
  4. The emissions comparison excludes vehicle/battery manufacture, upstream diesel production, transmission losses beyond the stated 90% charging assumption, and hourly grid-mix variation.
  5. Carrier proximity is prospecting evidence, not proof of route usage.
  6. All charging sites require trailer-access, queue, power-sharing, and PTV detour validation before an operating plan is approved.
